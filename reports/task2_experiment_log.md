# Task 2 Experiment Log

Task 1 combined masks are used as reference masks for the pixel metrics.

## Data split and duplicate review

- Ten user-identified images and one duplicate unlabeled image were excluded from the split. The source dataset remains unchanged.
- The first duplicate check used a perceptual hash. It grouped `not_damaged_27` / `not_damaged_78`, but missed the differently framed `not_damaged_135` / `not_damaged_145` pair. This led to the simpler full-image CLIP comparison.
- The split script extracts one normalized CLIP ViT-B/32 embedding from each full image and compares every pair with cosine similarity. Pairs at or above the selected cutoff are joined into similarity groups; transitive links form a single group. All retained members of a group go to development, and evaluation is selected only from singleton images.
- **Increment: label candidate pairs and compare cutoffs.** We visually labeled 28 high-similarity pairs (scores `>=0.950`) as same-scene or different-scene; the labels and brief reasons are in `reports/task2_clip_pair_review.csv`. On this reviewed set, cutoff `0.950` gave precision `0.9286`, recall `1.0000`, and F1 `0.9630` (26 same-scene pairs found, 2 different-scene pairs grouped). At `0.955`, F1 was `0.8511` (20 found, 1 different-scene pair grouped, 6 missed from the reviewed set). We selected `0.950` for the revised split because it had the best F1 on these candidates and retained all reviewed same-scene pairs. This recall is only within the reviewed high-similarity set; pairs below `0.950` were not comprehensively labeled.
- The selected `0.950` threshold groups 28 pairs into similarity groups. It catches the known `not_damaged_27` / `not_damaged_78` pair (`0.987935`) and the retained `not_damaged_124` / `not_damaged_135` scene (`0.978973`); the duplicate unlabeled `145` remains excluded. The review also found different-scene pairs `37/55` (`0.955334`) and `146/33` (`0.953865`), showing that CLIP can over-group. Groups are kept out of evaluation to favor leakage reduction.
- Similarity distribution analysis covered 5,995 pair scores: median `0.7914`, 95th percentile `0.8990`, and 99th percentile `0.9394`. Scores taper through the cutoff region with no clean separation, so we used the labeled candidate review to compare nearby cutoffs.
- Final split: 79 development images, 20 singleton evaluation images, and 11 excluded images. No CLIP similarity group crosses development and evaluation. The manifest records the groups and the ignored `outputs/task2/clip_pair_similarities.csv` records pair scores.
- The histogram and contact sheets are generated by `scripts/task2_similarity_analysis.py` and remain local under `outputs/task2/similarity_analysis/`. CLIP thresholding can over-group similar images and can miss duplicates below the cutoff. The Otsu results below were collected on the earlier development split and were not rerun after this split change.

### Trial: three overlapping crops

- **Change:** Along with the full image, extract three 75% crops positioned at the top-left, center, and bottom-right. For each image pair, combine full-image cosine similarity with the maximum crop-to-crop cosine similarity: `0.5 × full + 0.5 × crop maximum`.
- **Evaluation:** Use the same 28 labeled candidate pairs as the full-image method. The best crop score F1 was `0.963` at thresholds `0.900–0.930`, equal to the full-image F1 on this sample. At `0.930`, the crop score flagged 100 of 5,995 pairs and formed 14 groups; full-image CLIP at `0.950` flagged 28 pairs and formed 7 groups.
- **Decision:** The crops did not improve the reviewed-pair result and produced substantially more groups, suggesting more over-grouping. Keep full-image CLIP as the default split method; `--three-crops` remains available to reproduce this trial. The crop-pair scores are saved locally in `outputs/task2/clip_pair_similarities_three_crop_trial.csv`. No Task 2 split or Otsu result was changed for this trial.

## Trial 1: Global Otsu threshold on Lab lightness

**Reason:** Establish a fast, interpretable brightness-only baseline before adding local or shape-based rules.

**Method and parameters:** Convert each image to CIE Lab, apply binary Otsu thresholding to the L channel, and use the resulting mask as-is. No blur, morphology, contour filtering, or instance splitting is applied. Run on development images only.

**Development results:** 79 images; macro IoU `0.3346`, Dice `0.4568`, precision `0.3644`, recall `0.8859`. Mean Otsu threshold was `127.4` on the 8-bit L channel.

**Examples and shortcomings:** The isolated eggs in `not_damaged_148` (IoU `0.963`) and `not_damaged_115` (`0.953`) were segmented closely. In `not_damaged_24` (IoU `0.0004`), the bright wall was labeled as foreground and the egg was mostly missed. In `not_damaged_117` (`0.0216`), much of the egg carton was selected. The global brightness threshold finds many reference pixels but also includes bright background regions.

**Decision:** Keep as the baseline, not as the final method. Its high recall and low precision show that the next trial should use local image context.

## Trial 2: Gaussian adaptive threshold on Lab lightness

**Reason:** Test whether local brightness context can reduce the broad background detections seen with global Otsu thresholding.

**Method and parameters:** Apply Gaussian adaptive thresholding to Lab L using a `51 x 51` neighborhood and `C = -5`. No blur or morphology is applied. The threshold therefore varies with local brightness rather than using one image-wide value.

**Development results:** 79 images; macro IoU `0.0771`, Dice `0.1398`, precision `0.1624`, recall `0.1777`. The existing Otsu baseline scored IoU `0.3346`, Dice `0.4568`, precision `0.3644`, recall `0.8859`. Adaptive thresholding improved per-image IoU on 5 images and reduced it on 74. Otsu results were read from the prior run; Otsu was not rerun after the split change.

**Examples and shortcomings:** The method often labeled textured surroundings as foreground while missing egg interiors, visible as red false-positive regions and blue false-negative regions in the comparison overlays. It did not solve the bright-wall error in `not_damaged_24` or the carton error in `not_damaged_117`; overall, it lost too much egg coverage.

**Decision:** Discard this configuration. Local thresholding alone performs substantially worse than Otsu on this dataset, likely because egg surfaces are locally uniform and background texture creates many local intensity changes.

## Trial 3: GrabCut initialized from Otsu

**Reason:** Test whether GrabCut's foreground/background color models can refine Otsu's rough mask and remove background regions while retaining egg pixels.

**Method and parameters:** Convert Lab-L Otsu output into GrabCut labels: Otsu foreground pixels are probable foreground, a `5 x 5` eroded core is sure foreground, and other pixels are probable background. The image border (1% of the shorter dimension) is marked sure background. Run OpenCV GrabCut for 5 iterations and combine definite and probable foreground labels. No additional packages were needed.

**Development results:** Processed all 79 current development images. Macro IoU `0.3296`, Dice `0.4473`, precision `0.3433`, recall `0.9259`. The saved Otsu run predates the latest split, so the direct comparison uses only 76 image paths present in both results: Otsu IoU `0.3251`, Dice `0.4476`, precision `0.3512`, recall `0.8876`; GrabCut IoU `0.3290`, Dice `0.4465`, precision `0.3420`, recall `0.9314`. GrabCut improved IoU on 45 matched images and reduced it on 31. Otsu was not rerun.

**Examples and shortcomings:** GrabCut improved IoU for `not_damaged_33` (`0.693` to `0.864`) and `not_damaged_21` (`0.877` to `0.981`), but reduced it for `not_damaged_95` (`0.307` to `0.226`) and `not_damaged_62` (`0.134` to `0.079`). Overall gains are small and inconsistent: recall rises while precision and Dice fall slightly. The Otsu initialization still limits the color model when it incorrectly selects background or omits egg regions. Processing is also slower than direct thresholding.

**Decision:** Keep as a documented trial, but do not select it as the final method yet. It yields a small IoU increase on the shared comparison images, without a consistent improvement across metrics or examples.

## Trial 4: Random Forest pixel classifier

**Reason:** The threshold and GrabCut methods rely on per-image brightness or initialization. Train a classical classifier on color and local texture examples from development images so it can learn egg/background appearance across scenes.

**Method and parameters:** Extract Lab color, `9 x 9` local grayscale standard deviation, and grayscale gradient magnitude per pixel. Use 1,200 foreground and 1,200 background samples per training image after resizing images to at most 320 pixels on the long side. Train an 80-tree Random Forest (`max_depth=18`, `min_samples_leaf=4`, balanced subsample class weights). Remove connected components smaller than `0.01%` of image area (at least 16 pixels). A deterministic group-safe split uses 63 development images for training and 16 for validation; no image similarity group crosses the split. The evaluation split is untouched.

**Development results:** On the 16 validation images, macro IoU `0.3324`, Dice `0.4561`, precision `0.3853`, recall `0.7859`. On those exact same images, the saved Otsu results were IoU `0.2177`, Dice `0.3269`, precision `0.2286`, recall `0.9265`. The classifier improved IoU and Dice on 14 of 16 images and precision on 13; recall improved on 2. Otsu scores were read from the existing output and were not rerun.

**Examples and shortcomings:** `not_damaged_95` improved from IoU `0.307` with Otsu to `0.793`; `not_damaged_160` improved from `0.085` to `0.388`. `not_damaged_21` remained strong (`0.877` Otsu, `0.891` classifier). The classifier is more precise but misses more reference pixels overall, and a few validation images remain poor (for example, `not_damaged_116`, IoU `0.092`).

**Decision:** Keep this as the strongest current candidate. It improves the overlap and precision metrics substantially on the held-out development subset, while the lower recall shows that the `0.5` probability cutoff may be conservative. The split, features, and cutoff are fixed for this recorded trial.

## Trial 5: Group-safe out-of-fold threshold selection

**Reason:** The prior classifier used a fixed `0.5` probability cutoff and only 16 held-out dev images. Evaluate the precision/recall tradeoff across all development images while ensuring each image is predicted by a model that did not train on its similarity group.

**Method and parameters:** Assign CLIP similarity groups to five deterministic, approximately balanced folds. For each fold, train the same 80-tree Random Forest on the other four folds with 1,200 sampled pixels per class per training image, then save foreground probabilities for the held-out images. Evaluate thresholds from `0.15` through `0.80` in `0.05` increments, applying the same minimum-component cleanup (`0.01%` of image area, minimum 16 pixels). Choose the threshold with the highest macro IoU; ties are resolved by macro Dice, then closeness to `0.5`.

**Development results:** Out-of-fold predictions cover all 79 dev images. At threshold `0.50`, macro IoU `0.3928`, Dice `0.5303`, precision `0.4689`, recall `0.7641`. The selected `0.65` threshold gave IoU `0.4063`, Dice `0.5463`, precision `0.5438`, recall `0.6673`. The IoU improvement is `0.0135`; precision rises and recall falls. The untouched eval split was not used.

**Examples and shortcomings:** Increasing the threshold improves precision steadily over the tested range but reduces recall. This does not recover missed egg pixels; it selects a more conservative mask. Since the same out-of-fold predictions select and score the threshold, the best score may be optimistic from threshold selection. It is a development result, not a final eval estimate.

**Decision:** Keep `0.65` as the best development cutoff to test in the next comparison, but do not change the main classifier default or report it as final until the resulting masks are reviewed and then measured once on the locked eval split.

## Trial 6: Context-aware Random Forest

**Reason:** The first Random Forest used only per-pixel Lab color, local grayscale variation, and gradient. Add broader neighborhood context so predictions can use local appearance patterns at more than one scale.

**Method and parameters:** Extract per-pixel Lab color, local Lab mean and standard deviation over `9 x 9` and `31 x 31` neighborhoods, and grayscale gradient magnitude. Train the same 80-tree Random Forest with `max_depth=18`, `min_samples_leaf=4`, and balanced subsample class weights. Use 1,200 foreground and 1,200 background samples per training image, resized to a maximum long side of 320 pixels. Use five deterministic folds that keep each CLIP similarity group together. Test thresholds from `0.15` to `0.80` in `0.05` steps; remove components smaller than `0.01%` of the image area (minimum 16 pixels).

**Development results:** Out-of-fold predictions cover all 79 dev images. The selected threshold was `0.55`, giving macro IoU `0.4693`, Dice `0.6096`, precision `0.6132`, and recall `0.7019`. Compared with the previous pixel classifier's selected cutoff results (IoU `0.4063`, Dice `0.5463`, precision `0.5438`, recall `0.6673`), all four metrics improved. The evaluation split remains untouched.

**Decision:** Keep this as the strongest development candidate so far. The comparison is promising, but the selected threshold was chosen from the same out-of-fold predictions used to report these metrics, so the score may be optimistic. Evaluate it once on the locked eval split after the method is selected.

## Trial 7: Color-guided spatial refinement

**Reason:** Test whether local spatial consistency can further improve the context-aware Random Forest masks without adding a package.

**Method and parameters:** For each probability map, apply a joint-bilateral-style local weighted average over a `5 x 5` neighborhood. Spatial weights use sigma `2` pixels; color weights use Euclidean CIE Lab distance with sigma `18` on 8-bit channels. Threshold the refined probabilities at the context model's selected cutoff of `0.55`, then remove connected components below `0.01%` of image area (minimum 16 pixels). The same five group-safe folds and context-aware features are used. This is a dependency-free edge-aware approximation, not a DenseCRF implementation.

**Development results:** Out-of-fold predictions cover all 79 dev images. Macro IoU `0.4743`, Dice `0.6132`, precision `0.6186`, recall `0.7066`. The context-aware unrefined result at `0.55` was IoU `0.4693`, Dice `0.6096`, precision `0.6132`, recall `0.7019`.

**Decision:** The refinement gives a small improvement across all four metrics in this dev comparison. Keep it as a candidate; the gain is modest and was measured on the same folds used during development. Review overlays and confirm once on the locked eval split only after selecting the method.

## Trial 8: Weka-style multiscale Random Forest features

**Reason:** The context-only Random Forest improved over simpler methods, but local smoothing added little. Test a broader filter bank to give the classifier multiscale intensity, edge, structure, and texture cues.

**Method and parameters:** Compare three feature variants with the same 80-tree Random Forest, training pixel samples, 320-pixel maximum training side, and five group-safe folds: (A) Trial 6 context features, (B) multiscale features, and (C) context plus multiscale features. The filter bank uses Lab-L Gaussian smoothing at sigma `1`, `2`, and `4`, gradient magnitude, Laplacian, Hessian eigenvalues, and `9 x 9` local variance at each scale. All variants use threshold `0.55` and the same small-component cleanup. No new dependencies were installed; entropy and Gabor filters were not included in this first comparison.

**Development results:** On out-of-fold predictions for all 79 dev images, variant A scored macro IoU `0.4679`, Dice `0.6081`, precision `0.6122`, recall `0.7004`; variant B scored IoU `0.5193`, Dice `0.6424`, precision `0.6439`, recall `0.7081`; variant C scored IoU `0.5547`, Dice `0.6804`, precision `0.7080`, recall `0.7240`. The feature variants use the same folds and fixed threshold for a controlled comparison. The eval split remains untouched.

**Decision:** Keep variant C as the strongest Task 2 candidate so far. Adding the multiscale filter bank to context features improved all four metrics; B also outperformed A. These are development results and still need qualitative review before a final eval run.


## Qualitative review of Trial 8 variant C

**Reason:** Check where the strongest candidate fails before adding another algorithm.

**Method:** Recomputed per-image metrics from the saved variant C masks and reviewed the 12 lowest-IoU comparison overlays. No model was retrained and the eval split was not used.

**Development results:** Macro IoU `0.5547`, median `0.5647`; 16 of 79 images score below `0.30` and 15 above `0.80`. A few severe failures lower the mean.

**Failure types:**
- **Bright, cluttered background selected as egg:** `not_damaged_24`, `117`, `116`, `123`, `20`, `104`, `111`, `119` (IoU `0.02`-`0.26`). White walls, egg cartons, foil and skin are labeled foreground, and masks split into 30-110 fragments where the reference has one to three eggs. White eggs on white or shiny surroundings cannot be separated by local color and texture. In `24` the backlit egg is missed entirely.
- **Correct outline, hollow interior:** `not_damaged_141`, `35`, `37`, `100`, `34`, `101` (precision `0.84`-`0.99`, recall `0.37`-`0.60`). Eggs that fill the frame have featureless centers; the widest context window (`31 x 31`) and filter scale (sigma `4`) do not span them.

**Decision:** Keep variant C as the candidate. Both failure types suggest mask-level cleanup (hole filling and component filtering) before larger-scale features or Auto-Context.

## Trial 9: Mask cleanup on combined-feature probabilities

**Reason:** The qualitative review found fragmented background detections and hollow egg interiors; test whether mask-level cleanup fixes them without retraining.

**Method and parameters:** Retrain the combined context + multiscale Random Forest on the same five group-safe folds and threshold at `0.55` (Trial 8 variant C), then compare four variants: (A) baseline cleanup only; (B) fill enclosed holes; (C) morphological closing with an elliptical kernel of about `3%` of the shorter image side, then hole filling; (D) C plus removal of components smaller than `20%` of the largest or with mean probability below `0.6`. No new dependencies; the eval split was not used. The filter values were set once and not tuned.

**Development results:** Out-of-fold predictions for all 79 dev images.
- A: IoU `0.5546`, Dice `0.6802`, precision `0.7074`, recall `0.7241`.
- B: IoU `0.6058`, Dice `0.7176`, precision `0.7115`, recall `0.7958`.
- C: IoU `0.6111`, Dice `0.7188`, precision `0.6715`, recall `0.8650`.
- D: IoU `0.5884`, Dice `0.6775`, precision `0.6614`, recall `0.7695`.

Hole filling raised IoU on 53 images and lowered it on 8, none by more than `0.02`. Large gains came from the hollow-interior failures (for example `not_damaged_141`, `101`). A first run of this trial had a hole-filling bug (flood fill seeded at a foreground corner pixel turned some masks into the full image); it was fixed by padding the mask, and the numbers above are from the corrected run.

**Shortcomings:** Closing raises recall but merges nearby background, lowering precision, and hurts 23 images. The component filter removes genuine low-probability eggs (`not_damaged_34`, `37`, `138`) and is worse than hole filling alone. Remaining errors after hole filling: bright or textured background fragments (`not_damaged_149`, `30`, `27`, `36`, `32`) and large eggs whose interior holes connect to the boundary and are not enclosed (`not_damaged_27`, `36`, `118`, `119`).

**Decision:** Keep hole filling (B) as the cleanup step; it is the safest gain. Discard D. C has marginally higher IoU and Dice but lower precision and more regressions, so it is not selected.

## Trial 10: Scale-consistent Auto-Context

**Reason:** After hole filling, remaining failures were bright or textured background fragments and large-egg interiors. Checking the pipeline also showed a scale mismatch: all images are `640 x 640`, but every Random Forest was trained on pixels sampled from `320`-pixel images and applied at `640`, so window and filter scales differed by `2x` at test time. Auto-Context adds large-scale spatial context from a first-stage prediction.

**Method and parameters:** Train and predict at `320` pixels, then upsample probabilities to `640` for scoring against the reference masks. Stage 1 uses the combined context + multiscale features (Trial 8 variant C). Stage 2 is a second Random Forest (same settings) on those features plus the stage-1 probability map, its Gaussian blurs at sigma `4`, `8`, `16`, and a `31 x 31` local maximum. To avoid leakage, stage-1 probabilities for stage-2 training images come from an inner group-safe four-fold out-of-fold prediction inside each outer fold; the five outer folds are the same as before. Threshold `0.55`, small-component cleanup, then optional hole filling. The eval split was not used.

**Development results:** Out-of-fold predictions for all 79 dev images.
- Stage 1 at `320` (scale fix only): IoU `0.6461`, Dice `0.7509`, precision `0.7262`, recall `0.8540`; with hole filling IoU `0.6677`, Dice `0.7647`.
- Stage 2 (Auto-Context): IoU `0.7114`, Dice `0.7985`, precision `0.7788`, recall `0.8943`; with hole filling IoU `0.7176`, Dice `0.8020`, precision `0.7779`, recall `0.9036`.
- Previous best (Trial 9 hole filling at `640`): IoU `0.6058`, Dice `0.7176`.

With hole filling, the median per-image IoU is `0.81`; 41 of 79 images exceed `0.80` and 10 fall below `0.30` (previously 16).

**Examples and shortcomings:** The eggs are now found well, including large ones. The remaining low scores (`not_damaged_24`, `117`, `123`, `20`, `116`, `135`, `111`, `32`, `124`) have high recall but precision of `0.10`-`0.38`: large, smooth, bright regions such as white walls, cartons, a steel pot bottom and rim are labeled egg, because no pixel-level feature separates a white egg from a white surface. In `24` the backlit egg is mostly missed and the wall is selected. The threshold was fixed from earlier trials, but stage-2 settings were not tuned; scores remain development results.

**Decision:** Keep stage 2 with hole filling as the strongest candidate (`+0.11` IoU over Trial 9). The scale fix explains about `0.06` of the gain and Auto-Context the remaining `0.05`.


## Trial 11: Component-level shape filtering

**Reason:** After Auto-Context, the main remaining errors were large, irregular bright regions (walls, cartons, a pot) labeled as egg alongside correctly found eggs. Eggs are compact and convex, so component shape should separate them better than Trial 9's probability and size filter.

**Method and parameters:** Start from the Trial 10 stage-2 out-of-fold probabilities (threshold `0.55`, cleanup, hole filling) and extract connected components: `508` over the 79 dev images, `120` of them egg-like (at least `50%` of pixels inside the reference mask). Each component is described by area ratio, area relative to the largest component, solidity, extent, overlap with its fitted ellipse, circularity, aspect ratio, border contact, mean and max probability, and centroid. A `200`-tree Random Forest (`max_depth=5`, balanced class weights) predicts egg-like components, trained on the other folds of the same five group-safe folds. Components with keep probability of at least `0.5` are retained. A second variant also keeps the single highest-scoring component when none passes, since every dataset image contains an egg. No new dependencies; the eval split was not used.

**Development results:** Out-of-fold predictions for all 79 dev images.
- Auto-Context + fill (reference): IoU `0.7176`, Dice `0.8020`, precision `0.7779`, recall `0.9036`.
- Shape filter: IoU `0.7409`, Dice `0.8001`, precision `0.8195`, recall `0.8040`.
- Shape filter + keep best: IoU `0.7764`, Dice `0.8476`, precision `0.8692`, recall `0.8750`.

Keep-best improved IoU on 37 images and lowered it on 5. Median per-image IoU is `0.88`; 48 of 79 images exceed `0.80` and 5 fall below `0.30` (previously 10).

**Examples and shortcomings:** Many wall and carton false positives were removed. Remaining failures come from three causes:
- **Egg merged with a bright background region:** in `not_damaged_123`, `111`, `104`, `20` and `24` the egg and the wall form one component, which the shape classifier cannot separate (precision `0.14`-`0.35`).
- **Wrong or missing egg:** in `not_damaged_30` a white egg on a white wall is missed and the best-scoring component is a background speck (IoU `0.33` to `0.00`).
- **Multi-egg and partial eggs:** in `not_damaged_103` one of three eggs is discarded (`-0.15`), and `not_damaged_27`, `78` and `11` keep only part of a large egg (precision above `0.97`, recall about `0.4`).

The keep-best fallback assumes at least one egg per image, which may not hold for video frames. The keep probability `0.5` was fixed, not tuned, but shape-model and mask scores are still development results.

**Decision:** Keep the shape filter with keep-best as the strongest candidate (`+0.06` IoU, `+0.05` Dice over Auto-Context with hole filling). Report the keep-best assumption in the final method description.


## Final method and evaluation

**Reason:** Trial 11 is the strongest dev result and was selected as the final Task 2 method. The evaluation split had been run once earlier by mistake on a candidate pipeline; that run was discarded and no method choice or parameter was changed afterwards. The run reported here is the only evaluation result used.

**Method and parameters:** Trial 11 unchanged. Both Random Forest stages are trained on all 79 dev images at `320` pixels (stage-2 inputs come from five-fold group-safe out-of-fold stage-1 maps), probabilities are upsampled to `640`, thresholded at `0.55`, cleaned and hole-filled. The component shape classifier is trained on the dev out-of-fold components, keeps components with probability at least `0.5`, and falls back to the single best component when none passes. The `20` singleton eval images were not used for training, thresholds, or model choice.

**Evaluation results:** 20 images; macro IoU `0.8089`, Dice `0.8531`, precision `0.8457`, recall `0.8927`. Median per-image IoU is `0.93`; 16 of 20 images exceed `0.80` and 3 fall below `0.30`. For comparison, dev out-of-fold IoU was `0.7764` and Dice `0.8476`; the eval set contains 20 singleton images, so the difference is within what a small sample can produce and should not be read as an improvement.

**Examples and shortcomings:** Isolated eggs on plain backgrounds are segmented closely (IoU `0.94`-`0.99` for `not_damaged_19`, `140`, `156`, `15`, `110`, `127`). The four weakest images repeat the dev failure modes:
- **White egg on a white surface:** in `not_damaged_125` (IoU `0.08`) the egg is mostly missed and a white carton region is kept instead.
- **Egg plus bright background:** in `not_damaged_42` (`0.28`) the egg is found but carton-tray and rod fragments are added; in `not_damaged_112` (`0.70`) white bag material is attached to a correct egg.
- **Very large egg:** in `not_damaged_44` (`0.09`) a close-up egg held in a hand is mostly missed and the hand is selected.

The keep-best fallback assumes at least one egg per frame. Results use Task 1 masks as references, so errors in those masks carry into every score.

**Decision:** Task 2 is final with the Trial 11 pipeline. Its remaining weakness is separating white eggs from white surfaces, which pixel features and component shape do not resolve; it is reported as a limitation in the Task 3 comparison.
